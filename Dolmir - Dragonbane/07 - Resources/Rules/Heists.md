# Heists — Dragonbane Quick Reference

> [!info] House rules
> A Blades in the Dark-inspired framework for Dragonbane. Position, effect, clocks, bargains, flashbacks, and the resistance option below are house rules. Use normal Dragonbane rules for combat, damage, armor, conditions, WP, and pushing rolls unless stated otherwise.

## 1. The Plan & Gear

- Choose an approach: **Assault, Deception, Stealth, Magic, Social, or Transport**.
- Name the key detail: entry point, disguise, contact, route, target, or magical method.
- Declare carried equipment and use normal encumbrance. There are no abstract load slots; conspicuous weapons and heavy gear affect access and suspicion.
- Establish the objective and what success can realistically achieve before rolling.

## 2. Engagement Roll

The leader makes one relevant **skill roll** to determine the opening situation, not the outcome of the whole heist.

- **Boon:** A clear advantage in the plan or equipment especially suited to the approach. Do not count the same advantage twice.
- **Bane:** Strong security, poor information, unsuitable gear, or a major obstacle.
- Apply normal boon/bane cancellation and condition modifiers.

| Result | Starting situation |
|---|---|
| **Dragon (natural 1)** | **Controlled**, with an extra opportunity: ideal access, useful information, or an open escape route. |
| **Success** | **Risky**, with the plan underway; **Controlled** if the established circumstances already justify it. |
| **Failure** | **Risky** with an immediate obstacle, or **Desperate** if the obstacle is dangerous. |
| **Demon (natural 20)** | **Desperate**, with an immediate serious complication: alarm, ambush, separation, or blocked retreat. |

Do not allow pushing the engagement roll: it summarizes circumstances rather than a single retryable action.

## 3. Position & Effect

**Declare both before an action roll.** Position sets the danger; effect sets what success achieves. Neither changes the target skill value by itself.

### Position — How bad can it go?

- **Controlled:** Safe or advantageous. Consequences include delay, suspicion, lost opportunity, or a shift to Risky.
- **Risky:** Meaningful danger. Consequences include damage, exposure, lost equipment, or advancing a danger clock.
- **Desperate:** Severe danger. Consequences include serious damage, capture, separation, or loss of the objective.

Use actual Dragonbane damage and hazards, not Blades harm levels. Telegraph potentially lethal stakes before the roll.

### Effect — How much do you accomplish?

- **Zero:** No meaningful progress. Change the approach or secure an advantage before rolling.
- **Limited:** **1 segment** on a progress clock, or only part of the immediate objective.
- **Standard:** **2 segments**, or the immediate objective achieved.
- **Great:** **3 segments**, or the objective achieved with an additional benefit.
- **Extreme:** **4 segments**, only when exceptional fictional advantages justify it.

Do not require a clock for a simple obstacle that one successful action should resolve.

## 4. Action Roll Resolution

Roll **1d20 against the relevant skill**, using normal boons and banes.

| Result | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **Dragon (natural 1)** | Succeed with a bonus: increase effect one step, gain an opportunity, or improve the next position. |
| **Success (at or below skill)** | Achieve the declared effect without a new consequence from this roll. |
| **Failure (above skill)** | No progress and a consequence; **or offer success at a cost**, which the player may accept or decline. |
| **Demon (natural 20)** | Failure with a complication appropriate to the position. Cannot be pushed. |

**Success at a cost is the partial-success house rule:** for example, open the door but alert the guard, retrieve the ledger but leave evidence, or place the charge but lose the safe retreat. Do not invent a separate d20 partial-success band.

Failure should change the situation, not invite identical repeated rolls. Existing dangers and established costs still apply even on a success.

### Progress & Danger Clocks

- Use **4 segments** for a short obstacle, **6** for a substantial one, and **8** for a complex operation.
- Successful actions fill progress clocks according to effect.
- Consequences usually add **1 danger segment when Controlled, 2 when Risky, or 3 when Desperate**. Adjust for the actual threat.
- A filled danger clock triggers its named event; it does not automatically end the heist.
- Advance time-based clocks when time genuinely passes, not automatically after every roll.

## 5. Pushing, Assistance & Bargains

### Push a Roll

Use normal Dragonbane pushing rules: after a failed eligible roll, take an appropriate condition not already marked and reroll. No pushing demons or rolls the normal rules prohibit. The condition has its normal attribute-linked bane; pushing does not grant extra effect.

### Assistance & Setup

- Use normal helping rules when direct assistance is possible.
- A separate setup action can improve the next action's position or effect if it changes the situation: distract a guard, acquire a key, or brace an escape route.
- Roll a setup action only when its outcome is uncertain and failure matters.

### Devil's Bargain — House Rule

Before rolling, accept a definite complication for **one boon**: leave identifying evidence, owe a contact a favor, sacrifice equipment, or advance a danger clock.

- The bargain happens whether the roll succeeds or fails.
- It cannot be resisted or undone by pushing.
- A bargain cannot force another player character's decision without their player's agreement.

## 6. Resistance — Optional House Rule

When a consequence is announced, the GM may offer one immediate resistance attempt if a plausible response exists.

- Describe the response and roll a relevant **attribute**: AGL to catch a ledge, CON to endure fumes, WIL to hold your nerve, etc.
- **Cost: 2 WP**, paid before rolling. No attempt without sufficient WP.
- **Success:** Reduce the consequence one step; negate it only if the fiction allows.
- **Dragon:** Negate it if possible, otherwise reduce it substantially.
- **Failure:** The original consequence stands; WP remains spent.
- **Demon:** The original consequence stands; no additional punishment is needed.
- One attempt per consequence; no pushing resistance rolls.

Resistance cannot reverse an established event, create impossible movement, or cancel a devil's bargain. Do not add this roll on top of normal armor, parries, dodges, or another roll already used to resolve the same threat.

## 7. Flashbacks — House Rule

Declare plausible preparation without stopping for a long planning scene.

- **0 WP:** Ordinary, obvious preparation with no meaningful obstacle.
- **1 WP:** A simple arrangement: hide a rope, scout an exit, or arrange a signal.
- **2+ WP:** Complex preparation involving restricted access, a risky contact, or several steps. Agree on the cost first.
- Pay normal money, equipment, and other resource costs as well.
- Roll a relevant skill if the preparation was uncertain. WP buys the opportunity, not automatic success.
- Flashbacks cannot contradict established facts or erase something that has already happened. A concealed tool must still fit the character's carrying capacity.

## 8. Payoff & Heat

- Award the agreed money, items, information, favors, or faction leverage. Use normal Dragonbane advancement.
- Track **Heat** as an optional **6-segment attention clock for the relevant faction**, not a universal wanted level.
- Suggested additions: **+1** for witnesses or suspicious evidence; **+2** for identification or a conspicuous intrusion; **+3** for a public disaster directly linked to the crew. Choose the best fit rather than stacking every description of the same incident.
- When full, the faction acts: investigation, checkpoints, questioning contacts, arrest attempts, or retaliation. Start a new clock only if further escalation makes sense.
- Heat belongs to whoever is implicated. Characters do not gain Heat merely because an unrelated operation happened nearby.

## Score Flow

1. **Brief:** Establish the objective, opposition, and stakes.
2. **Plan & gear:** Choose approach, key detail, and equipment.
3. **Engagement:** Roll for the starting position.
4. **Action loop:** Describe action → declare position/effect → roll → resolve progress and consequences.
5. **Adapt:** Use assistance, setup, pushing, bargains, and flashbacks.
6. **End:** Escape, withdrawal, capture, or a settled objective ends the score.
7. **Aftermath:** Resolve rewards, casualties, evidence, Heat, and faction reactions.

## Example: Thabor & the Red Ravens Against the Shelob

### Off-Screen Score

- Players may roll the Ravens' dice, but **use NPC skill ratings, not the absent PCs' skills**.
- Give the operation's leader and specialists appropriate ratings before rolling. If no statistics exist, **12 for competent operatives and 14 for a specialist** are suggested house-rule defaults.
- Make an engagement roll, then one roll per phase: **place charges → detonate → escape**. Choose skills based on the actual method; use a declared specialist rating if no existing skill fits explosives.
- For this compressed NPC operation, omit WP, resistance, pushing, and flashbacks. Preparation and equipment provide fictional advantages and boons as appropriate.

### Danger Clocks

- **Shelob closes in — 4:** It attacks and blocks the planned escape.
- **Tunnels destabilise — 4:** A collapse endangers the crew and threatens occupied areas above.

Advance these through consequences, using the position guidelines. Their completion changes the operation rather than automatically declaring everyone dead.

### Establish the Charges' Effect

- **Limited:** Injure or bury the Shelob; death is not confirmed.
- **Standard:** Kill it if the established placement and explosives are sufficient.
- **Great:** Kill it and destroy the nest.

A successful detonation delivers the agreed effect. A failed escape does not undo a successful kill; it determines the cost to the Ravens.

### Show the Aftermath

- Intercut tremors, fleeing vermin, and the blast with the PCs' current scene.
- PCs roll for their own rescues and responses, not the Ravens' competence.
- Survivors, wreckage, and any remains reveal what happened. Thabor claims credit for whatever he can plausibly present as a victory.
