The Green Wyrd

"Every creature that bleeds has a weakness. Every plant that grows has a purpose. The art is knowing which is which before it matters."

The Art

The Green Wyrd is old knowledge — older than the Faith, older than the Umbarian throne, older than most of the names currently attached to things. It passed through Eldar hands for generations before the Faith decided that knowing too much about the body's workings was a form of heresy. Now it survives in fragments: in the margins of confiscated texts, in the memories of hedge-witches, in the quiet expertise of those who learned it from someone who learned it from someone who remembered.

It is built on observation, preparation, and the willingness to put dangerous things into your own body in service of a result. Its practitioners are not reckless. They are precise. The difference matters, because the line between a curative and a poison is almost always dosage.

Anima walks this path not because she chose it as a discipline but because she was raised inside it. For her, the Green Wyrd is not a school. It is a first language.

Masters of the Wyrd

The Green Wyrd does not have formal masters in the way a fencing school does. Knowledge passes between practitioners as trust allows — a recipe here, a technique there, a warning about what happens if you get the ratio wrong. Anima's advancement comes not from finding a teacher but from deepening what she already knows: pushing into more dangerous territory, working with components she has not used before, accepting the cost of that knowledge in her own flesh.

Unlock: Anima begins the path already. Tier 1 is available from the start of play, gated only by the skill requirement and the availability of ingredients. Tier 2 requires her to have worked with monster components at least once in play — to have harvested something from a creature she helped bring down and used it deliberately. Tier 3 requires something she cannot seek out: a moment where the Wyrd cost her something real and she continued anyway.

Each ability requires a shift to learn and a roll at session's end. Roll under Healing to succeed.

Ingredients

The Green Wyrd uses two categories of ingredient. What Anima can make depends on what she has.

Common components — foraged herbs, roots, bark, fungi, and plant matter. Available whenever the party passes through wilderness, farmland, or anywhere living things grow. Anima may attempt a WITS roll when the party rests in appropriate terrain to restock. On a success she replenishes her common component supply. On a failure, the terrain didn't yield what she needed.

Rare components — parts harvested from monsters and creatures: blood, bile, venom glands, organs, bone marrow, and similar materials. These are obtained only by deliberately harvesting them from slain creatures. Harvesting requires a Healing roll and takes ten minutes. Rare components are specific — venom from a viper is not interchangeable with bile from a marsh creature. Anima tracks what she has.

Heroic Abilities

Field Brew
Tier 1 | 2 WP | Healing 13 | Common components | One action

Anima prepares and applies a swift brew from whatever she is carrying. Choose one effect when activating:

  • Staunching Draft: One target within reach stops bleeding immediately and regains 1d4 HP. Does not cure wounds — only halts loss.
  • Clarity Tincture: One target shakes off a single ongoing condition — frightened, confused, or similar. Does not remove conditions caused by magic or curses.
  • Numbing Compress: One target ignores the penalty from one injury until the end of the scene. The injury is still there. They simply stop feeling it. There is a cost to this — when the compress wears off, the target suffers a bane on all actions for one round as the pain returns.

Each use consumes common components. Anima tracks her supply.

The Study
Tier 2 | 2 WP | Healing 15, Field Brew | Common or Rare components | Rest before encounter

Anima has learned to read an enemy's physiology and prepare accordingly. When the party knows — or reasonably suspects — what they are about to face, Anima may spend a rest preparing a targeted compound.

Roll WITS during preparation:

  • On a success with common components only: The compound is effective but incomplete. Choose one benefit below, applied at half effect.
  • On a success with rare components relevant to the creature type: Full effect. Choose one benefit below.
  • On a failure: The preparation is wasted. Components are lost.

Benefits (choose one):

  • Weakening Oil: Applied to one character's weapon before combat. Attacks against the target creature deal +1 damage die for the scene.
  • Warding Salve: Applied to one character's skin or armour. The first time that character takes damage from the target creature type this scene, reduce it by 4.
  • Tracker's Smoke: A slow-burning bundle that, when lit, causes creatures of the target type to become uneasy. Affected creatures suffer a bane on attacks against the character who lit it for two rounds.

Preparation is specific. A compound made for wolves does not work on men. A compound made for the living does not work on the dead.

Master Technique

Decoction
Tier 3 | 3 WP | Healing 17, The Study, narrative gate | Rare components (specific) | Full rest required

Anima distils a true decoction — a concentrated preparation that temporarily rewrites how her own body functions. This is not medicine. It is transformation at cost.

During a full rest, Anima may prepare one decoction using rare components. She chooses the effect. The decoction lasts until the end of the next significant encounter or scene, then fades.

Decoction effects (choose one per preparation):

Swallow's Draught (requires: predator venom or similar)

Anima's reactions sharpen to an inhuman degree. She gains a boon on all Dodge rolls and acts first in initiative regardless of card draw. At the decoction's end she must pass a CON roll or suffer the Exhausted condition.

Griffin's Reduction (requires: creature with unnatural resilience — troll bile, stone-hide matter, similar)

Anima's body hardens temporarily. Reduce all incoming damage by 2 for the duration. While active she moves at half speed and suffers a bane on AGI-based rolls. At the decoction's end she loses 1d4 HP as the strain settles into her body.

Ekimmara's Extract (requires: blood-drinking creature — the component must be fresh)

Each time Anima deals damage during the duration she recovers HP equal to half the damage dealt, rounded down. While active her eyes darken visibly and she cannot be mistaken for fully human. At the decoction's end she must pass a WIL roll or become ravenously hungry — she cannot benefit from rest until she eats a full meal.

A decoction cannot be given to another character. It is brewed for Anima's specific physiology. What it does to her it would do differently — and likely worse — to someone else.

The Wyrd Cost

The Green Wyrd at its deepest level is a negotiation with the body. Field Brew costs components. The Study costs preparation time and components. Decoction costs both — and asks something of Anima herself each time she uses it.

This is intentional. The Faith brands this knowledge heresy partly because they are right that it is dangerous. Not to others. To the practitioner. Every decoction Anima brews is a small experiment in how far her body will go before it stops coming back.

For the GM: Track the Wyrd cost across sessions. A character who uses Decoction frequently without consequence is not walking the path — they are ignoring it. Occasional complication, rare escalation, a moment eventually where the body says no more tonight — these are not punishments. They are the texture of this particular expertise.

Design Notes

Preparation as player skill: The party that thinks ahead about what they're walking into gets more out of Anima than the party that improvises. Field Brew covers improvisation. The Study rewards foresight. Decoction rewards willingness to pay.
Calibrated costs: The decoction drawbacks are genuinely inconvenient rather than punishing: Exhausted, half-speed, ravenously hungry. They don't end sessions. They shape the session after the one where she used them — felt later, not immediately.
Eldar nature separate: Anima's Eldar heritage is left untouched here deliberately. The Green Wyrd is about her craft, not her blood. If her Eldar heritage develops separately, the path remains compatible with whatever that becomes.
