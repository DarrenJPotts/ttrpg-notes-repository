### A Heroic Path for Dragonbane

> *"Every creature is a language. Bones remember. The book speaks. And what it speaks, the beast understands."*

---

## The Art

The Bone Tongue is not a taught practice. It awakens in those who touch both magic and hunger simultaneously — who learn to speak to spirits through the pieces they keep. For Clawbert Danfangle, a wolfkin merchant with a collector's instinct that borders on predation, the Bone Tongue began the moment the book's whispers aligned with his hunger: *Every part taken is a word. Every bone a sentence. Listen.*

It is built on **Animism** (hearing spirits, communing with beasts) and **the Collection** (harvested parts used as focuses, anchors, and trophies). What makes it dangerous is that the collection is not separate from the magic — the hunger feeds the magic, and the magic feeds the hunger. They reinforce each other. To walk this path is to accept that Clawbert is becoming something that wears a merchant's face.

The book knows this. The book approves.

---

## The Corruption Gate

The Bone Tongue has no external master. It awakens when two things align:

1. Clawbert learns **Animism magic**
2. He takes a piece of something living—not by accident, but by **deliberate choice**

Once both happen, Tier 1 becomes available immediately.

**Tier 2** requires him to have used his collection in magic at least once—to speak through a bone, to wear a hide, to invoke a spirit *through* something he took. This deepens the bond between hunger and magic.

**Tier 3** requires a moment of **genuine corruption**: using the path to cause harm (not self-defense, but predation), witnessed or discovered by the party. The threshold is crossed. Clawbert has hunted. And the party knows.

**The GM tracks this progression.** Unlike other paths, the Bone Tongue advances through **moral choice**, not mechanical achievement. This is intentional.

## Heroic Abilities

### Speak Through the Pieces
- **Requirement:** Animism 13
- **Willpower Points:** 2
- **Focus:** One item from the collection (bone, claw, tooth, hair, hide)
- **Activation:** One action in combat, or minutes outside it

Clawbert activates one piece from his collection and speaks to the spirit bound within it. Choose one effect when activating:

- **Beast's Senses:** Clawbert temporarily sees/hears through the creature that piece came from. For one round, he gains a **boon** to Awareness and can see in darkness as that creature could. The sensation is *visceral* — he experiences hunger, predatory instinct, the urge to hunt.

- **The Hunter's Mark:** Clawbert calls down the rage of the creature whose part he holds. One target within sight suffers a **bane** on all actions until the end of the scene as the spirit hounds them. Clawbert experiences the target as *prey*.

- **Whispered Hunger:** The book speaks through Clawbert. He may ask the spirit a single question about something it knew in life. The answer is truthful but *twisted* — filtered through predatory instinct and hunger. (Example: "Where is the merchant hiding?" answered as "Where the weak things huddle to feel safe.")

*Each use adds another piece to the collection's weight. The more pieces, the louder the whispers.*

---

### The Harvest
- **Requirement:** Animism 15, Speak Through the Pieces, the collection contains at least 5 significant pieces
- **Willpower Points:** 3
- **Focus:** A living target (willing, unwilling, or dying)
- **Activation:** One action in combat, or negotiation outside it

Clawbert's animism deepens into something feral. He moves with unnatural speed and focus, drawn to a specific part of a target's body — hair, nails, teeth, skin, blood. Roll **Brawling**:

- **On success:** He takes what he wants without killing the target (usually). The piece goes into the collection. The target suffers **one condition of your choice** (Frightened, Wounded, or Sickly depending on what was taken).

- **On a Dragon roll:** He takes the piece *and* the target is marked. Any **Speak Through the Pieces** casting using this new piece automatically succeeds and comes with a boon. The target feels the mark — knows they are hunted.

- **On failure:** The target resists or escapes. Clawbert is left with bloodlust unfulfilled, suffering a **bane** on all rolls until he rests or takes something from *someone*.

*This ability reveals what Clawbert is becoming. It is not subtle. It is not defense. It is predation. The party will see it. They will know.*

## Master Technique

### The Book's Answer
- **Requirement:** Animism 17, The Harvest, corruption gate (predation witnessed/discovered), narrative moment of no return
- **Willpower Points:** 3
- **Focus:** The bone artifact from the Old God site (or another significant collection piece)
- **Activation:** Full ritual (takes a full rest, requires isolation or willing participants)

Clawbert opens himself to the book's whispers and the spirits in his collection. For the next significant encounter or scene, the boundary between Clawbert and the collection blurs. He is no longer primarily a merchant.

During this time:
- All **Animism** spells cast gain a **boon** and cost 1 fewer WP
- Clawbert can sense all living creatures within 50 meters
- When he attacks with The Harvest or natural weapons, he may choose to take a piece instead of dealing damage (add that piece to the collection *instantly*)

**The cost:**
- Clawbert cannot distinguish between enemies and allies. Anything that moves and breathes is *prey*. The party must physically restrain or redirect him, or he will hunt them.
- After the scene ends, Clawbert suffers **Exhaustion** and must eat raw meat (not prepared food) to recover his senses
- If he cannot hunt or feed, he becomes increasingly unstable and **suffers a bane on all actions** until satisfied

*This is the threshold of the path. Not a triumph. An apotheosis into something dangerous. The table should understand: this is how Clawbert falls. And whether they save him or stop him is no longer guaranteed.*

## The Collection As Mechanic

Clawbert's collection is not flavor. It is **game-relevant and dangerous**.

**Pieces in the collection:**
- Merry's claw (wolfkin, prized — whispers constantly)
- Hermit's hide (preserved as hat/cloak)
- The Old God bone (artifact, whispers suggestions for *what's missing*)
- Smaller pieces: teeth, nails, hair, bones from various creatures and people

**When using Bone Tongue abilities:**
- More pieces = stronger whispers but greater compulsion to hunt
- Removing pieces from the collection creates genuine withdrawal (bane on all rolls until the hunger is satisfied)
- The book catalogs the collection and whispers suggestions for *what else he needs*

**Party intervention — critical to the narrative:**
- If they discover/destroy the collection, Clawbert suffers genuine trauma (condition: Broken, unable to use Bone Tongue until addressed through roleplay or magic)
- If they help him *understand* his collection's weight, it becomes a redemption hook
- If they enable him to keep building it, he drifts further into corruption
- If they let him reach Tier 3 unprepared, they may not like what happens next

## GM Notes on Running This Path

**The Bone Tongue is a path toward damnation or redemption.**

It is not a power fantasy. It is a warning.

**Progression feels like a descent:**
- **Tier 1** (Speak Through) — Clawbert explores his gift. It feels intoxicating. He whispers to bones. Cool. Powerful. What's the harm?
- **Tier 2** (The Harvest) — The line between magic and predation blurs. He *takes* pieces. Small ones at first. Then not-so-small. An NPC notices something is missing. Then another. The party becomes aware of what he's doing.
- **Tier 3** (The Book's Answer) — Full corruption. He is no longer Clawbert. Something else is wearing his skin. If the party lets this happen, they own it. They chose to keep traveling with a thing that hunts.

**The party can:**
1. **Help him break the cycle** — destroy the collection, exorcise the book, accept him anyway (difficult, rewarding arc)
2. **Arrest or exile him** — turn him in, separate him from the collection (tragic, but just)
3. **Enable it** — use his power, accept what he's becoming, become complicit (dark campaign, intentional)
4. **Tragic ending** — let him fall fully, face the consequences (he becomes an enemy, hunter, monster)

**Clawbert should feel like a descent.** Each tier is more corrupted, more feral. The magic works beautifully and *horrifyingly*. The party realizes their companion is becoming a monster, and they must decide if they save him or stop him.

**The collection grows.**  Every piece taken is a failure of restraint. Track them publicly. Let the party see how many pieces are in there now. Let them do the math on what kind of person collects this much.

## Design Notes

The Bone Tongue is deliberately the *darkest* of your heroic paths. It sits in contrast to the others:

- **Gentle Blade** = mastery through restraint
- **Twin Shadow** = power through partnership
- **Green Wyrd** = power through knowledge and preparation
- **Drake's Oath** = power through sacrifice and commitment
- **Bone Tongue** = power through corruption and hunger

Where the others have external gates (masters, bonds, narratives), the Bone Tongue's gate is *internal* — Clawbert's own choices. This makes it the player's responsibility to keep him human. If they stop paying attention, if they stop questioning what he's doing, he slides.

Tier 3 is deliberately limited compared to the initial concept. It does not grant all-spells-boon or break action economy. But it *is* powerful, and it *is* transformative. The cost (cannot distinguish allies/enemies, needs to hunt, becomes unstable) should create genuine table tension.

Use this path carefully. It's a character study waiting to happen. It's also a cautionary tale about what happens when you travel with something that hunts.
