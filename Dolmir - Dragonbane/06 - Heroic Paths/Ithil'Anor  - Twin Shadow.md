A Heroic Path for Dragonbane

> *"One blade, two sides. One must shine for the shadowed edge to strike."*
---
## The Art

Created by the twin Elve'than warriors of the Mittland. Ithil of the Moon and Anor of the Sun. It is said the two would eclipse the battlefield, the one would be the blinding light of the sun while the other hides in it's shadow. 

The First Long War brought desolation to the Mittlands and Eldar, and the Twin Shadow's walked in its wake. Ithil and Anor were two sides of one blade and the blade can only be wielded by the two who are one 

---

## The Bond

Ithil'Anor - Twin Shadow is a **shared path**. Both characters must learn each ability together. When one advances, both advance. Neither can use a Ithil'Anor - Twin Shadow ability without the other present and willing.

**Unlock:** No external master is required. The path awakens through play, specifically, through moments where the two characters act in concert under genuine pressure and succeed. The GM marks these moments. When enough have accumulated, the first ability becomes available. 

---

## Heroic Abilities

### The Sun Shines while Shadow Cuts
- **Requirement:** Any weapon skill  13 (both characters)
- **Willpower Points:** 2 each
- **Range:** Adjacent — both characters must be within melee range of the same target

Just as the Twin Warriors, Ithil and Anor, one of the bonded pair creates an eclipse, an opening to a target enemy; drawing their attention while the other strikes in tandem. 

Activate on that character's turn. The second character, **regardless of initiative order**, may immediately perform a free attack against the target. This attack is made with a **boon**.

The first character does not attack. Their action is the distraction, it is spent whether or not the second character's attack succeeds.

*The target must be capable of being distracted. This does not work on mindless creatures or those immune to feints.*

---

### Switching
- **Requirement:** Any weapon skill or Brawling at 15 (both characters), The Opening
- **Willpower Points:** 2 each
- **Range:** Within sight of each other

Activate at any point during either character's turn, before actions are declared. The two characters **swap positions instantly** — each moves to where the other was standing. Neither movement provokes a free attack.

Following the swap, both characters gain a **boon** on their next action this round if they attack the same target.

*This ability requires no words. It is a look, a shift of weight, a thing learned so deep it has stopped being a decision.*

---

## Master Technique

### One Body
- **Requirement:** Any weapon skill or Brawling at 17 (both characters), Switching, bond forged through significant shared hardship in play
- **Willpower Points:** 3 each
- **Range:** Within sight — no adjacency required

Activate on either character's turn. Until the start of that character's next turn, the two fight as a single coordinated entity. During this time:

- When one character is attacked, the **other** may use their reaction to attempt a Dodge or Parry on their behalf, as if they were the one being struck.
- When one character succeeds on an attack, the other may immediately make a **free attack** against the same target as a reaction — this does not consume their turn.
- If both characters attack the same target in the same round while One Body is active, the target suffers a **bane** on all actions until the start of the next round.

One Body ends immediately if the two characters are separated by more than their line of sight, or if either is incapacitated.

*This is a passive window, not a single action. It reshapes the round for both of them.*

---

## The Bond Gate

The Master Technique cannot be unlocked through training alone. The GM decides when it becomes available, based on what has happened between the two characters in play. It requires a moment — not a mechanical trigger, but a narrative one — where the bond between them is tested at real cost and holds.

They will know it when it happens. So will the table.

## Design Notes

The Twin Accord is built around a deliberate tension: the abilities are strongest when the two characters are together, which means separating them is a genuine tactical threat. An enemy who understands what they are watching will try to split them. This should feel like a vulnerability as much as a strength.

The path intentionally has no external master because Lia and Lily's bond is the source — importing a teacher would dilute that. The unlock-through-play mechanism means the GM controls pacing naturally, without needing to gate it behind a quest or an NPC.

Lily's changeling nature is left deliberately untouched here, per the original design intent. If her shapeshifting develops as character growth rather than as heroic abilities, the Twin Accord remains compatible with whatever she becomes — the path is about the bond between them, not about what either of them is.
