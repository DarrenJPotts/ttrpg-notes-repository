The Twin Accord

"One shadow. Two edges."

The Art

The Twin Accord is not taught by a master in the traditional sense. It grows between two people who have fought together long enough that their bodies begin to anticipate each other — who has learned the rhythm of the other's breath, the weight of their hesitation, the exact moment they are about to move.

Most warriors who develop this bond do so by accident, over years. The bond between Lia and Lily is not accidental. It is blood, and something stranger than blood. Their coordination is not a technique — it is a conversation that has been running since before either of them had words for it.

This path is written for them. Another pair could walk it, but they would need something comparable: shared history, shared survival, and enough trust that one partner's body answers before the other's mind has finished asking.

The Bond

The Twin Accord is a shared path. Both characters must learn each ability together. When one advances, both advance. Neither can use a Twin Accord ability without the other present and willing.

Unlock: No external master is required. The path awakens through play — specifically, through moments where the two characters act in concert under genuine pressure and succeed. The GM marks these moments. When enough have accumulated, the first ability becomes available. The characters do not seek this out. It finds them.

Each ability still requires a shift to bed in and a roll at session's end. Both characters roll — both must succeed before the ability is fully learned. If one fails, neither gains it yet.

Heroic Abilities

The Opening
Tier 1 | 2 WP each | Weapon/Brawling 13 (both) | Range: Adjacent

One character moves to draw the target's full attention — a feint, a shout, a sudden press. Activate on that character's turn. The second character, regardless of initiative order, may immediately perform a free attack against the target. This attack is made with a boon.

The first character does not attack. Their action is the distraction — it is spent whether or not the second character's attack succeeds.

The target must be capable of being distracted. This does not work on mindless creatures or those immune to feints.

Switching
Tier 2 | 2 WP each | Weapon/Brawling 15 (both), The Opening | Range: Within sight

Activate at any point during either character's turn, before actions are declared. The two characters swap positions instantly — each moves to where the other was standing. Neither movement provokes a free attack.

Following the swap, both characters gain a boon on their next action this round if they attack the same target.

This ability requires no words. It is a look, a shift of weight, a thing learned so deep it has stopped being a decision.

Master Technique

One Body
Tier 3 | 3 WP each | Weapon/Brawling 17 (both), Switching + narrative gate | Range: Within sight

Activate on either character's turn. Until the start of that character's next turn, the two fight as a single coordinated entity. During this time:

  • When one character is attacked, the other may use their reaction to attempt a Dodge or Parry on their behalf, as if they were the one being struck.
  • When one character succeeds on an attack, the other may immediately make a free attack against the same target as a reaction — this does not consume their turn.
  • If both characters attack the same target in the same round while One Body is active, the target suffers a bane on all actions until the start of the next round.

One Body ends immediately if the two characters are separated by more than their line of sight, or if either is incapacitated.

This is a passive window, not a single action. It reshapes the round for both of them.

The Bond Gate

The Master Technique cannot be unlocked through training alone. The GM decides when it becomes available, based on what has happened between the two characters in play. It requires a moment — not a mechanical trigger, but a narrative one — where the bond between them is tested at real cost and holds.

They will know it when it happens. So will the table.

Design Notes

The Twin Accord is built around a deliberate tension: the abilities are strongest when the two characters are together, which means separating them is a genuine tactical threat. An enemy who understands what they are watching will try to split them. This should feel like a vulnerability as much as a strength.
No external master: The path intentionally has no external master because Lia and Lily's bond is the source — importing a teacher would dilute that. The unlock-through-play mechanism means the GM controls pacing naturally, without needing to gate it behind a quest or an NPC.
Lily's changeling nature: Left deliberately untouched here, per the original design intent. If her shapeshifting develops as character growth rather than as heroic abilities, the Twin Accord remains compatible with whatever she becomes — the path is about the bond between them, not about what either of them is.
