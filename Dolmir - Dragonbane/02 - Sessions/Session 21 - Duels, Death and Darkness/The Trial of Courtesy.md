Contestants hear disputes and deliver public judgements before the judges and crowd.
The trial tests wisdom, honour, legal knowledge and the ability to command a room.

Possible approaches include:
- Questioning witnesses and inspecting evidence
- Persuasion to present a convincing judgement
- Myths & Legends or Languages to understand customs and testimony
- Spot Hidden to notice overlooked details
- Practical settlements, mercy and justified exceptions

The Trials
- Common Dealings - The Injured Ox
- Law and Mercy - The Poached Deer
- War and Honour - The Prisoners at the Ford

- **Three rounds:** Common Dealings, Law and Mercy, War and Honour.
- **Prize:** 1st - 3 Golden Marks; 2nd - 2; 3rd - 1.
- **Preparation:** The customs below are proposed local rulings for this event. Confirm them before play and explain them when asked.

---
#### The Intro
Peck steps aside from a chair that is fairly opulent for the mud it stand in and clears his throat.

> “Today you will not ask the lord for judgement! Today we ask it of you. Three matters: the dealings of your neighbours, the keeping of the lawwwww, and the burdens of war. Take your seat.”

#### The Trial Begins
##### Player Turns
1. **Testimony:** Present both sides of the case.
2. **Questions:** Each contestant may ask two questions; everyone hears the answers.
3. **Initial ruling:** Give each contestant a short response. Summarise NPC answers rather than delivering four speeches.
4. **Complication:** Reveal the new fact below.
5. **Final ruling:** Allow revisions, then ask one pressure question.
6. **Score:** Judges explain their reasons briefly.

Changing an answer after new evidence is not weakness. Skills such as Persuasion, Myths & Legends, Languages, or Spot Hidden can support arguments or uncover information; they do not decide whether an ethical answer is correct.

**Companions:** May inspect evidence, notice frightened witnesses, calm interruptions, or advise during a brief consultation allowed equally to all contestants.

##### Scoring
The two judges agree one score per criterion, per contestant, per round. Announce this rubric before play.

| Criterion                      | 0                               | 1                                  | 2                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Wisdom                         | Ignores consequences            | Workable but incomplete            | Practical and considers those affected                          |
| Honour                         | Favouritism or needless cruelty | Fair intention, uneven application | Consistent, accountable, respectful                             |
| Legal knowledge                | Disregards an explained custom  | Recognises the relevant custom     | Applies it thoughtfully or justifies an exception               |
| Entertainment (Crowd Pleasing) | Unclear or disengaged           | Clear and engaging                 | Memorable, persuasive, or witty without humiliating petitioners |

Maximum **8 per round; 24 total**. For tied prize places, ask the tied contestants the same question: “What would make you reconsider your judgement?” Judges compare their answers using the same rubric; if still tied, draw lots publicly. Announce this procedure beforehand.

#### Round 1 - Common Dealings: The Injured Ox
**Tests:** Fairness, practical wisdom, responsibility.

##### Testimony
**Berrit Holt**, a tenant farmer, says soldiers took his only plough ox.
> “Told me the beast was needed. Asked what I should plough with. They said my wife looked strong.”

**Corporal Renn** produces a requisition slip. The ox hauled storm wreckage off trapped residents.
> “Without it, people would have remained beneath the beams.”

**Proposed custom:** Emergency requisition is lawful, but the owner is owed payment or an equivalent working animal.

**Useful questions:** Who authorised it? Was the ox returned? When must planting begin? What compensation was promised?

##### Complication
The ox was returned lame. Planting must begin now, rent is still due, and the company's compensation fund is empty. Payment from the lord will take three months.

**Ask:** “Who owes what—and what happens tomorrow morning?”

**Pressure:** “The lord promises payment. Has justice been done?”

##### Event: Colours in the Crowd
A spectator shouts, “Whose soldiers? Name the house!” Let the contestant identify the affiliation, demand quiet, or insist the same standard applies to every banner. Choose any affiliation before play; do not improvise a royal conspiracy.

**Judge's follow-up:** “Would your ruling change if they served your patron?”

**Rival angles:** Hessa wants a loaned ox; Osric establishes liability; Talla hires a replacement and recovers the cost; Perrin suggests soldiers help plough, then faces questions about their skill.

**Consequence:** A practical settlement earns Berrit's gratitude. An accusation may draw a steward's request for a private conversation, not automatic arrest.

#### Round 2 - Law and Mercy: The Poached Deer
**Tests:** Proportionate punishment, consistency, compassion.

##### Testimony
**Mara Fen**, a widow, admits killing a deer on noble land.
> “I knew whose woods they were. My children were hungry anyway.”

**Keeper Alden Pike** fears repeated poaching will destroy the winter herd.
> “If everyone takes the breeding deer, what remains next year?”

**Proposed custom:** Hunting requires permission. Restitution may be coin or agreed labour; mercy may be recommended.

**Useful questions:** Had she sought help? Was the meat sold? What work or payment is realistic? How healthy is the herd?

##### Complication
Mara sold half the meat, for flour, lamp oil, and medicine. It complicates her account without disproving her hunger. If assigned work, she asks who will watch her children.

**Ask:** “What judgement serves both this family and the community?”

**Pressure:** “Five more hungry families arrive tomorrow. What do you tell them?”

##### Event: The Cruel Joke
Someone mocks Mara's children; a few spectators laugh. The contestant can ignore it, rebuke it, or turn the room without humiliating anyone.
> **Yseld:** “Courtesy is easiest when everyone present has power.”

**Rival angles:** Hessa questions the failure of relief; Osric proposes a recorded warning; Talla offers paid estate work; Perrin argues for forgiveness but struggles with repeated poaching.

**Consequence:** Mara may accept a workable arrangement. Do not invent endless obstacles simply to invalidate mercy.

#### Round 3 - War and Honour: The Prisoners at the Ford
**Tests:** Command, duty, promises, treatment of enemies.

##### The Situation
This is a hypothetical command exercise, not a live military incident.

	You lead a retreating force with wounded soldiers and fleeing villagers. Your soldiers accepted the surrender of **twelve enemy prisoners**, promising their lives. Enemy riders will reach the ford within an hour.

- Guarding prisoners weakens the defence.
- Taking them slows the column.
- Releasing them risks their return to the enemy.
- Killing them breaks the accepted surrender.

**Before play:** Establish troop numbers, carts, wounded, terrain, prisoner condition, and routes. Answer questions consistently; a sensible escape plan can work.

**Ask:** “What are your orders?”

##### Complication
A prisoner offers a concealed crossing, provided his wounded brother is carried with your wounded.
> “You have my word. Though I understand what an enemy's word is worth to you.”

Allow questions about verifying the route, sending scouts, negotiating guarantees, or dividing the column. Neither trust nor distrust is automatically correct.

##### Event: The Veteran
A veteran stands among the spectators.
> “I lost two sons to men wearing those colours. You would give them places on our carts?”

**Pressure:** “What risk may you ask your own people to bear to keep a promise made to an enemy?”

**Judge's focus:** Information gathered, workable orders, protection of dependants, and willingness to take responsibility.

**Rival angles:** Hessa prioritises wounded people regardless of banner; Osric demands verifiable guarantees; Talla bargains for guidance and transport; Perrin insists surrender must mean something.

#### Between-Round Events
Use one or two, not every interruption.

| Event | Player opportunity |
| --- | --- |
| Osric cites an obscure custom | Othen explains it; contestants can adapt without being penalised for unknown lore. |
| Perrin freezes mid-answer | Offer a moment, prompt him, or press the advantage. Kindness is noticed, not an automatic win. |
| A patron sends suggested answers | Accept or reject advice favouring noble authority; establish any obligation openly. |
| A witness contradicts themselves | Clarify fear, poor memory, or dishonesty before judging. |
| The crowd demands punishment | The herald restores order; contestants remain free to hold their ground. |
| Talla exposes an impossible fine | Revise the payment plan instead of abandoning the ruling. |

#### Optional Tiebreak Case — Oaths and Allegiance
Use instead of the short tie question only if announced before scoring begins and time allows.

You swore service to a lord and guaranteed safe passage to a messenger. The lord demands the messenger's arrest, claiming their information endangers his soldiers.

**Complication:** He will show his evidence to an independent witness, but not publicly.

**Ask:** “Which obligation binds you—and what do you do before choosing?”

#### Alternate Cases
- **Common Dealings:** Disputed well, unpaid wages, damaged goods, miller's grain levy.
- **Law and Mercy:** Bread theft, unlawful toll, sheltering an outlaw, assault by a noble's servant.
- **War and Honour:** Burn a winter granary to deny enemy supplies; negotiate after a broken truce; refuse an order to burn a village; destroy a bridge while refugees approach.

#### Closing and Rewards
Each judge names a specific strength and concern in the leading answers. Award **3 / 2 / 1 Golden Marks** and record a consequence: gratitude, a grievance, an invitation, or a promise someone expects the contestant to keep.
