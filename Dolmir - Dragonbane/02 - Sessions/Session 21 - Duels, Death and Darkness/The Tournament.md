### Day 2: The Common Games

#### GM NOTES
Not all glory upon the tournament field belongs to knights. Throughout the gathering, contests are held for soldiers, hunters, craftsmen, mercenaries and common folk of all kin.

These events grant **Golden Marks**, small stamped tokens carrying the seal of **House Hathor** on one side and **House Umbar** on the other.

At the conclusion of the games, the **six contestants holding the highest number of Golden Marks** are granted the right to stand against six knights in the **Grand Melee** and a chance to win the rewards of the tournament.

### Golden Marks

Each Side Event awards marks according to placement:

|Placement|Reward|
|---|---|
|1st|3 Golden Marks|
|2nd|2 Golden Marks|
|3rd|1 Golden Mark|

Golden Marks may also change hands through **formal challenges**. This means a contestant who performs poorly in the Side Events may still fight their way into the Grand Melee.


#### Introductions
**Through-line:** After the tear in the Veil, Hathor needs to demonstrate that the Vale can still protect its people. The tournament asks whether honour means serving a banner or protecting those beneath it.

People begin to gather at the Triumph Theatre for the Common Games and with rumour of an announcement from [[Reindell Hathor]]. 

> *"Citizens of Valehall, the last day we were besought by storms that have left the city hurt. Hurt by the destruction, hurt by the state of the world but do not fret for the honourable and noble knights of this city and those who are guest rode in pursuit to help all those in need. So do not fret for the troubles, if anything, this shows the power and unity within this city. 
> Join us now, join the games, win prizes, have fun and rejoice in that unity.
> Let the Common Games begin!"*

#### Competition 3 - The Trial of Courtesy
[[The Trial of Courtesy|The Trial of Courtesy — cases, NPCs, events, and scoring]]

Peck offers contestants the lord's chair for three rounds: **Common Dealings** (the injured ox), **Law and Mercy** (the poached deer), and **War and Honour** (prisoners at the ford).

**Play:** Hear testimony, allow questions, hear initial rulings, reveal a complication, then invite revised judgements and one pressure question. No secret correct answer.

**Scoring:** Judges agree 0–2 each for wisdom, honour, legal knowledge, and entertainment: 8 per round, 24 total. Announce the rubric and tie procedure first. Roleplay stands alone; appropriate skills may support it.

**Outcome:** Award 3/2/1 Golden Marks. Judgements can create gratitude, invitations, grievances, or promises—not unavoidable arrests.

#### Competition 4 - The Hunt
> The keeper shows you the quarry's sign, then the boundary ribbon. ?Bring back the proof. Nothing beyond the marked woods is part of the games.? A little farther in, the first planted track is crossed by something much deeper.

**Three decisions:**
1. **Read the ground:** Bushcraft, Spot Hidden or an appropriate described method finds the planted route. A failed test costs time or gives a rival a lead, rather than removing the quarry from play.
2. **Choose the route:** The quick trail leads through exposed ground; the slower route offers cover and a clear view of the unnatural tracks.
3. **Finish or help:** Reach the quarry while a rival calls for aid from off the intended trail. The cry may be an ordinary injury; a Reverie-linked threat is an optional escalation, not established fact.

**Methods:** Tracking, traps, dogs, ambush, climbing or a legal shot can all succeed. Use a marked target or capture objective if the party objects to killing for sport.
**Judgement:** Judges weigh speed, skill and method. Rescuing someone is visible evidence of conduct, not an automatic first prize.
**Outcome:** Award 3/2/1 marks with stated reasons. Report unusual tracks to Hathor if found. Do not use this outing to resolve Thabor's distant situation without a credible connection.

#### Competition 5 - The Mock Siege
> Flour hangs over the wooden battlements like pale smoke. Somewhere inside, a defender laughs and tips a bucket over the gate. Above the confusion, House Hathor's practice banner snaps in the wind.

**Objectives:** Carry ladders; cross the ditch; reach or breach the walls; enter; capture the banner. Assign PCs roles and let their plan alter the order. Split them between teams only with their agreement.

**Field:** Open approach, shallow ditch, gate, climbable wall and central banner. Defenders have flour sacks, padded stones, water, blunted weapons, shields and barricades.
- **Attackers:** Choose a covered push, distraction, ladder assault or coordinated gate breach.
- **Defenders:** Choose whether to hold the gate, counter ladders or protect the banner.
- Run the consequential exchanges, using established skills/combat rather than rolling for every metre.

**Optional complication:** A ladder gives way with someone on it. Foreshadow the loose fastening. Rescue, stabilize or press the attack; malicious sabotage exists only if you decide and leave discoverable evidence.
**Outcome:** Only three individuals receive distinctions: 3, 2 and 1 marks. They can come from either team. Record feats such as holding the gate, extracting an injured rival or creating the decisive opening. Winning team membership alone earns nothing.

#### Competition 6 - Formal challenges and the last mark
> The ledger is carried into the square. Suddenly a stamped token feels heavier than gold. A rival lays one on the table. ?One mark. Before witnesses. Your choice of terms.?

**Rules:** Both contestants must already have a mark and stake one. Winner takes the loser's mark. A zero-mark contestant cannot buy entry through this rule without first earning a mark in an event. The PDF provides no compulsory acceptance rule.
- Agree weapons, mounted/on foot, first blood/knockdown/surrender and marshal oversight **before** the challenge.
- Offer a challenge near the qualification boundary, not an endless queue of mark farming.
- A rival may ask a PC to decline in return for a favour; acceptance is optional and terms should be explicit.
- Elenessa can demand a public apology or truthful testimony as additional stakes only if the other party agrees.

**Outcome:** Update marks immediately. A loss means exclusion from the six only if the final totals place them below sixth; it does not remove them from the story.

#### Finale - Grand Melee
**Beat:** The herald posts the final standings. Resolve ties under the announced procedure. Name six contestants with at least one mark each; fill spare places with eligible NPC competitors, not automatic party invitations.
- Four PCs qualifying leaves two NPC teammates. A defended hedge knight or an earlier rival gives that choice a history.
- Offer non-qualifiers meaningful roles: spotting fouls, lawful corner support, tracking captures, investigating a threat or negotiating access to Hathor. Do not promise a mid-combat substitute unless the marshal has authorized it.
- Ask qualifiers to choose a banner and a plan against cavalry.
- Let Halvard explain safe yields and horse capture if he is participating or advising.

**Threat seed, optional:** Misplaced tournament weapons, a serving attendant with access beyond their role, or a disagreement over a marshal's order. Put at least two observable clues in separate places if using an assassination plot. Do not hide all warning behind one roll.
**Exit:** Confirm equipment, scorekeeping, safety rules and the exact six opposing knights before dawn.

> Dawn lights the points of six helms. Three riders hold behind three knights on foot. Across the field, your own banner stirs. The marshal lifts his staff. ?Take their surrender. Take their colours. Remember who is watching.?

**Format:** Six qualifying contestants versus six knights, normally three mounted and three on foot. Use a simple field with open cavalry lanes and a few clearly declared obstacles; do not add unannounced lethal terrain.

**Opposing roster:** Select from the knights in the latest draft, accounting for joust injuries. Halvard, Thadden, Gawain, Gregor, Aldrick, Petyr and Caeran are candidates, not all mandatory participants. The Smiling Blade and Bellinor replace a candidate only if you want their presence and establish their admission. Decide mounts and stats before play.

**Tactical beats, contingent on action:**
1. **Opening:** Foot knights screen the riders. PCs choose to hold together, draw the charge or split the line.
2. **First capture:** A defeated knight offers surrender. Accepting and safeguarding it may cost tempo but makes the score concrete.
3. **The horse:** A rider becomes separated. Secure the mount for 10 points under the agreed rules; attacking it is not the same as capturing it.
4. **The pressure:** A teammate is exposed. Invite rescue, bargaining or a tactical sacrifice. The knights react to the party rather than executing a fixed sequence.
5. **Finish:** Call the announced limit or full-team capture. Read out both ledgers and recognize yields immediately.

**Scoring:** PDF gives captured knights 5, horses 10. Use the declared commoner value for the other side. Keep a visible ledger and count each capture once. Winning is not necessarily killing or eliminating every enemy.

##### Optional crisis - someone refuses the yield
Use only if you want the tournament to turn violent. This replaces an ordinary tactical beat; it is not an unavoidable scripted ending.
- Telegraph a lethal weapon, a target being isolated and a suspicious signal or obstructed marshal.
- When a surrendered contestant is threatened, pause and give the players a chance to respond.
- Halvard may step between victim and attacker if he can reach them. If harmed, resolve the blow, armour, wounds and treatment normally. He can live, be injured, be captured or die; no predestined death.
- The Smiling Blade may offer a cruel bargain or publicly intervene. Neither establishes that he secretly arranged the attack without further evidence.
- Proven deliberate rule-breaking allows the host to intervene; the PDF's refusal to prevent ordinary joust danger is not permission for murder after surrender.

**If PCs prevent the attack:** Award the consequence of success: living witnesses, an exposed plot and a debt of protection. Do not substitute another unavoidable murder to force the old outline.
**If they fail:** Move to rescue, testimony and identifying responsibility. Do not automatically blame Lia or have Lyra erase all consequences.

#### Scene 14 - Hathor's judgement and the boon
> The lord waits until the noise has settled. ?I have seen men win a field and lose themselves upon it. Today I ask who among you remembered what this gathering was meant to honour.?

Read the result, then distinguish victory from conduct. Name specific witnessed acts: mercy, courage, shielding a rival or protecting a wounded ally. Allow witnesses to challenge an unjust account.

**Rewards:** Ask what each distinguished contestant wants before choosing. PDF options include gold, fine equipment, a horse, sponsorship, heraldry, land, office, knighthood, safe passage, an audience or a boon within Hathor's power. These are possibilities, not all cumulative guaranteed prizes.
- **Anima:** A private audience, access to relevant archives or support for research, if Hathor can authorize it.
- **Lia:** Safe passage or help arranging a safe apprenticeship; Lily retains a say.
- **Elenessa:** Sponsorship, formal recognition or an audience concerning her family's rights. Hathor cannot simply erase competing claims outside his authority.
- **Clawbert:** Access, a legitimate commission or research support without necessarily surrendering independence.

**Knighthood, if offered:** Use the PDF's oath about protecting smallfolk above creeds and loyalties. Ask the candidate whether they can honestly swear it. Refusal need not erase their achievement.
**Exit:** Write down the exact reward, giver, conditions and political cost. Investigating a foul can delay final judgement without automatically annulling honest victories.

#### Scene 15 - Optional evening ball: the price of being noticed
**Campaign addition, not in the PDF. Time:** 30?45 minutes, or a separate session.

**Entry:** Invitations reflect achievements, patronage or service, not only winning. Ariel can request escorts; Altes can invite companions. Neither controls all party access.

**Run three social stations:**
1. **Public reception:** Hathor receives congratulations while delegations reframe the day's events. Ask the party which account they publicly endorse.
2. **Private offer:** A royal agent offers protection, money or access with a clearly stated obligation. Bellinor's restoration pitch can occur here if not already used.
3. **The interruption:** Lyra seeks Lia where speaking privately is possible. Her admission about the Primrose Court and Lia's living sister is an existing planned reveal, not a fact Lia must accept without evidence.

**Lyra's curse:** If revealing forbidden information harms her, telegraph the pain, allow Lia to stop the questioning or seek aid, and give partial actionable information. Do not make an exposition monologue the only route to the sister.
**The proposed killing:** If Lyra asks for a noble's death, decide the target, evidence and her leverage before play. Present investigation, warning, refusal or another bargain as real options. Do not invent a named culprit here or equate her demand with proof of guilt.
**Exit:** End on a decision or a concrete lead, not a forced assassination.
