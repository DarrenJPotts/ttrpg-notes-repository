The sign above the door is painted in faded gold lettering, a pair of scales beneath the name with a single eye at the centre. From inside the sound of something being moved, a scrape of furniture on stone, the occasional quiet word that might be someone talking to themselves.

When the party pushes the door open they find a boy on his hands and knees collecting scattered papers from the floor. The shop behind him is in the particular state of a room that was tidy before someone decided it should not be. Shelves emptied, drawers pulled out and left where they fell, a small display case cracked along one side. He has made a start on putting it right but there is a long way to go.

He looks up. He is perhaps fourteen, slight, ink on his fingers and dust on his knees.

_"We are not open,"_ he says. Then, after a pause in which he appears to reconsider: _"But I suppose you can come in."_

**Secrets and Clues**
- His name is **Pip Giles**. He has been Jefferson's apprentice. He is good at his job and he knows it and right now that is the only stable thing in his world
- Jefferson came to the shop three days ago in a hurry. Not his usual manner, Jefferson was a methodical man, a planner, not someone who arrived breathless and left faster than he came
    - He took something from the back room. Pip does not know what. Jefferson told him to go home early and not come back until morning
    - _"He said it like he was doing me a favour. I think he was."_
- When Pip arrived the next morning the door was already open. The shop was like this. Jefferson was gone
    - Nothing of obvious value was taken — the coin box was untouched, the display stock intact. Whoever came was looking for something specific
    - They did not find it. Pip knows this because the hidden drawer under the counter, the one Jefferson showed him in his first week and told him never to mention, is still closed. They missed it
- The hidden drawer contains a small oilskin package that Jefferson left there some weeks ago with instructions that it was only to be opened if he did not return from a journey
    - Jefferson left on no journey Pip knows about. He is not sure the instructions apply. He has been staring at the drawer for two days
    - If the party convinces him to open it: inside is a folded document, a key on a cord, and a name written on a card — **Elliot Lovecraft** — with an address in the lower district that turns out to be a building that burned down six months ago
- Pip does not know who Elliot Lovecraft is. The name means nothing to him. He offers it freely because he has nobody else to give it to
- Jefferson said one other thing before he left that morning, quietly, more to himself than to Pip:
    - _"I should not have looked. But I could not have not looked."_
    - Pip has been turning that sentence over for three days. He still does not know what it means
