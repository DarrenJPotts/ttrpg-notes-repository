#### The Third Leg - Tower Pass
The road through the Tower Pass is little more than a memory carved into the mountainside. What began as broken stone paths soon gives way to steep slopes of jagged rock and narrow ledges slick with rainwater. The further upward you climb, the quieter the world becomes. Even the sound of rainfall feels distant beneath the constant whistle of wind threading through the peaks.

The Vale now rests far below you.

From the heights, Ashveil and its vineyards are little more than faint smudges of colour swallowed by mist and stormcloud. The mountains ahead rise endlessly, their dark silhouettes vanishing into sheets of grey fog. At times the path narrows so tightly the cart wheels scrape stone with every turn, the drop beside you disappearing into pale nothingness.

The air grows colder with each passing hour.

Small cairns of stacked stone begin appearing beside the trail. Old things. Ancient enough that moss has claimed them entirely. Some are decorated with hanging charms made from bone, antlers and faded cloth that sway softly in the wind. No one knows who placed them there.

As dusk begins to settle over the mountains, a shape slowly emerges through the fog ahead.

A watchtower.

Or what remains of one.

The old ruin clings to the mountainside overlooking the pass, half-collapsed and weathered by centuries of storms. Its upper floors have long since fallen away, leaving only a broken stone shell wrapped in creeping ivy and blackened pine roots. Part of the outer wall has collapsed entirely, exposing the hollow interior to the wind.

The horses grow restless as you approach.

The wind howls through the hollow cracks of the tower walls, producing a low mournful sound that almost resembles distant voices. Somewhere deeper in the fog beyond the tower, something metallic clinks softly against stone.

Not once.

But rhythmically.

Like someone walking just out of sight.
