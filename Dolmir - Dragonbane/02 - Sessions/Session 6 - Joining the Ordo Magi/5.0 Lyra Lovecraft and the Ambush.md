##### Notes
- The party is surrounded, there is no way out. 
- They can attempt to talk but it will not change the outcome
- They have a choice when engaging, lethal or non lethal. Most of what they have is farmer equipment except a few who are geared. 
- Some look scared now that combat is escalating, some look unsure if they should be there but the chaos ensues 

---

**Initial Combatant Mix**

**Start with ~12 people in/around the fight:**
- **3-4 Inciters** (armed, better trained, leather armor, actually dangerous)
  - These are the ones who *organize* the sympathiser mobs
  - They have weapons (clubs, short blades)
  - They actively escalate violence
  - They won't back down easily
  
- **6-8 Regular Protestors** (angry but untrained)
  - Armed with improvised weapons (sticks, rocks, fists)
  - Zealous but disorganized
  - Will flee if cornered or if their leader falls
  - Can be intimidated or subdued without lethal force
  
- **2-3 Innocent Bystanders** (civilian merchants, workers, etc.)
  - Caught in the wrong place at wrong time
  - Not looking for a fight—want to leave
  - Can be accidentally caught in combat
  - If killed/seriously injured, it has consequences (guilt, witness reports, party reputation)

---

**The Moral Choice**

Party can fight **lethal or non-lethal**, but both approaches have consequences:

**If Party Goes Lethal:**
- Inciters recognize this and escalate (drawing actual weapons, fighting harder)
- Regular protestors may panick or fight with more ferocity
- Innocents try desperately to flee or hide
- If any innocent is killed, survivors **will report it** to authorities
- Sympathy for the party drops; guards take the incident more seriously

**If Party Goes Non-Lethal:**
- Takes longer, requires more precision
- Protestors can still be subdued through grappling, intimidation, or non-lethal strikes
- Inciters will *exploit* the party's restraint—they'll fight dirtier, target vulnerable party members, grab innocents as shields
- Innocents are safer but the fight is messier and longer
- Party gains reputation as merciful (some factions respect this)

---

**Combatant Behavior (Fluid)**

- **Inciters:** Fight to win. If they realize they're losing, they try to rally remaining protestors or escape.
- **Regular Protestors:** Follow the Inciter's lead. If Inciter is down, they're confused and vulnerable. Many flee at first sign of real danger.
- **Innocent Bystanders:** 
  - First round: Try to get away from the fight (retreat to edges, hide behind stalls)
  - If cornered or grabbed: Panic, cry for help, might swing in self-defense
  - If party protects them: They remember this, might give testimony later
  - If party ignores them: They scatter, some get caught in crossfire (possibility of accidental harm)

---

**Combat Flow**

**Round 1-2:** 
- Inciters and a few protestors engage party
- Innocents trying to flee
- Other protestors sizing up the fight (some join, some wait to see)
- **GM calls:** *"Merchant tries to run past you toward the side street. Do you stop them or let them go?"*

**Mid-Combat (Round 3-4):**
- Numbers flux: Reinforcements arrive (maybe 2-3 more protestors), or some are subdued and removed from fight
- Innocents are mostly gone (fled or sheltering)
- Inciters realize they're losing OR party is struggling, adjust tactics
- **Escalation point:** If party is clearly winning, protestors flee. If party is struggling, Inciters get more aggressive.

**End Combat:**
- Once Inciters are down or most protestors flee, the rest scatter
- If any Inciters are captured/subdued, they're valuable for interrogation
- Combat ends when enemies are no longer a threat, not necessarily when all are dead

---

**Tactical Complications**

- **Narrow alley setting:** Limiting space favors the party (hard for reinforcements), but innocents can't flee easily
- **Market stalls/obstacles:** Combat is chaotic. Can overturn stalls for cover or use them to block escape routes
- **Civilian collateral:** Missed swings, spell effects, etc. can hit bystanders. Party must be careful.
- **Noise draws attention:** Guards might arrive mid-combat or immediately after (depending on location)

---

**Example Inciter Stats** (adjust per your rules):
- **The Leader** (scarred man, short sword, leather armor): Trained fighter, yells orders, stays in the fight
- **Muscle** (2 others with clubs/spears): Angry and effective, protect the leader, disengage if leader falls
- **Other Protestors:** Untrained, high morale while leader is alive, break when scared

---

**Non-Lethal Options**

Party can:
- **Grapple** and pin protestors to remove them from fight
- **Intimidate** (especially lower-morale protestors—"Stand down or get hurt")
- **Disarm** rather than kill
- **Subdue with magic** (sleep spells, etc.)
- **Create barriers** to separate innocents from the fight
- **Flee** if overwhelmed (combat doesn't have to end in victory)

---

**Innocent Mechanics**

If a party member attacks near an innocent:
- Call for a check (Dex, Stealth, or Insight) to see if innocent is hit
- If hit (failed check): Innocent is hurt, scared, will report party as violent
- If party helps innocent escape/protects them: Innocent becomes potential ally/witness/refuge later

---

**Aftermath Depends on Party Approach**

- **Lethal, killed innocents:** Authorities take it seriously. Investigation. Reputation damage.
- **Lethal, no innocents killed:** Sympathisers are dead. No witnesses. Clean resolution (for a street fight).
- **Non-lethal, protected innocents:** Survivors report party as protective. Reputation boost in lower quarter.
- **Non-lethal, didn't care about innocents:** Messy, people hurt anyway. Mixed reputation.

---

**Lyra's Escape**

Somewhere during the fight—probably around mid-combat—the party catches sight of her.

**She's on the rooftops**, silhouetted against the sky. She pauses for just a moment, looking *down* at Lia (if Lia is visible, fighting). There's that same expression again—longing? Guilt? Then she *runs*, disappearing across the rooftops toward the upper quarter.

The rooftops are too high and too dangerous for most to follow safely. If a party member tries, they're abandoning the ground fight, and their allies are in trouble.

---

**Aftermath**

- The sympathisers scatter once they realize they're losing or once Lyra is clearly gone (their apparent mission: "stop the Eldar conspiracy" has failed)
- Local guards arrive *after* the fight (typical for the lower quarter)
- Lia is left with: she saw her mother, she was close enough to recognize her, but not close enough to speak. Lyra *saw* her too. There was a moment of connection.
- The party is bloodied, alive, but they didn't stop Lyra. She's still out there. Still distributing parcels.
