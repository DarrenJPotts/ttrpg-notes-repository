#### **GM Guidance: Running the Black Market**

**Tone & Atmosphere:**
- Empty, but *not silent*—water sounds, faint voices, echoes that don't quite make sense
- The compulsion is *emotional*, not magical (yet)—people didn't fight it because they *wanted* to follow
- Every discovery should raise the stakes: this isn't just a rescue mission, it's a moral and supernatural trap

**Pacing Notes:**
- Don't rush the investigation. Let the party explore and piece things together.
- If they skip areas, the Compulsion Clock starts ticking faster (water gets wrong quicker)
- The note from Kess and the letter about Jefferson are *keystone moments*—let them land
- The gateway should feel like a choice, not an inevitability (even though opening it is probably inevitable)

**Using NPCs Effectively:**

**Corwin (The Fence):**
- Use him as a mirror for the party's fears. He's not afraid of them; he's afraid of *what's coming*
- His blindness is meaningful—he can't see the gateway, but he can *feel* it
- Dialogue should be sparse and meaningful. Silence is more unsettling than explanation
- He won't help them escape or fight. He'll only tell them what he knows.

**Thabor (The Mercenary):**
- Use him as a pressure valve. If the party needs a fight before the gateway, Thabor is there
- He's not evil, just *employed*. If they talk to him, he's surprisingly honest (the wine helps)
- His fear of the voices is genuine. He's been alone too long.

**Nisala (The Imprisoned Elf):**
- Don't make her a quest marker. Make her a *mirror of what's possible*
- If the party opens the gateway without understanding Miriel, Nisala shows them the endgame
- Her barely-sane state is a warning: the voice consumes people

**The Shambler:**
- He's not a fight, he's a *symptom*
- If the party kills him out of fear or mercy, that's a moral choice that matters
- If they help him, he might provide crucial info (in broken fragments) about what he heard

**What the Party Should Learn (Order Flexible):**
1. Jefferson was deliberately sabotaged (Letter)
2. There's an organized slave trade (Cells, ledger)
3. Something called Miriel is trapped behind the gateway (Locket, Nisala, Corwin)
4. Someone powerful *wanted* the seal broken (Corwin's revelation)
5. Opening the gateway will have personal costs (the Compulsion Clock, the voice's pull)

**If the Party Opens the Gateway:**
- Describe it as *relief*. Something that's been pushing all this time suddenly gives way.
- The water behaves strangely (rises, or falls, or both)
- The voice becomes almost conversational: warm, grateful, but also *needy*
- Miriel is grateful. She's also been alone. She will ask them for help.
- This is the hook for deeper Underlanes content.

**If the Party Refuses to Open the Gateway:**
- The compulsion intensifies
- Nisala will try to open it herself (forcing their hand)
- The Shambler might become violent (command to protect the seal? Against opening it?)
- Corwin will reveal his final secret: he was ordered to *let them* open it
- *The gate will eventually open itself. Question is whether they're inside or outside when it does.*
