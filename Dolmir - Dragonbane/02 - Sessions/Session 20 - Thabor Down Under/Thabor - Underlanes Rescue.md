### 3. The Damaged Junction

A collapsed beam divides the passage. Water slips beneath it. Three taps answer three taps from beyond the rubble.

- Establish communication before requiring a roll. Thabor can describe the rising water and the wounded Raven.
- A crawlspace permits contact and supplies but not immediate evacuation of the injured survivor.
- Removing the beam widens access but requires support; the danger is obvious from the sagging ceiling.
- A side drain provides another route if traced or cleared.

**Problems to solve:** Stabilise the opening, deal with water and arrange a way to carry someone who cannot walk. They need not solve these in that order or use a particular skill.

### 4. The Survivors
Thabor is trying to keep everyone above the water and conserve their lamps. He has abandoned the original attempt to secure territory: getting people out is now his priority.
- The wounded Raven needs stabilisation and assisted transport, not a special ingredient.
- The other Raven can help move equipment and explain how the water is entering.
- Thabor wants the dead recovered, but will accept a plan to return rather than demand an obviously suicidal delay.
- The dead Shelob and intact pockets of its nest are accessible through a side opening. Salvage is optional, visibly dangerous and unnecessary for Pip.

Give the survivors tasks if the party establishes communication: tie a rope, stop a leak, brace a shelf, relay tapping. Successful preparation should make extraction easier.

## Pressure — Chamber Flooding

Use a **four-step clock**. Tell the players the water is rising and describe every change. Start at 0 when the hook arrives unless established elapsed time justifies otherwise.

Advance for a substantial avoidable delay, a failed risky operation with an established flooding consequence, or leaving the situation unattended for a significant period. Do not advance for ordinary discussion, every roll, or separately for simultaneous tasks. Warn players before a proposed lengthy task will advance it.

1. **Seepage:** Water covers the floor; the survivors move supplies upwards.
2. **Rising:** The wounded Raven must be lifted onto a ledge. The original crawlspace begins taking water.
3. **Critical:** Water reaches the ledge. Thabor signals that they must move; heavy salvage is abandoned.
4. **Chamber lost:** The survivors retreat through a higher service opening, leaving gear and the dead behind. The original rendezvous floods; rescuers must redirect to the higher passage. Anyone still physically trapped there faces an immediate, clearly described drowning danger, resolved in play rather than automatic death.

The higher opening should be visible on the messenger's sketch or mentioned by Thabor before the final stage. It leads deeper, not straight to safety.

**Counterplay:** Diverting the inflow stops the clock while the diversion holds. A sound engineering solution can drain the chamber or eliminate the pressure altogether. Stabilising masonry prevents collapse consequences but does not automatically stop water. Reward the actual solution rather than preserving the clock artificially.

## Secrets & Clues

Place these wherever the party investigates; none depends on one successful roll.

- The explosion struck a methane pocket: charred stone and the survivor's account establish an accident, not sabotage.
- Thabor's tapping confirms survival before a dangerous commitment is required.
- The water enters through a ruptured drain, not the nest; its flow can be redirected upstream.
- The collapsed wall has exposed older worked stone and a sealed branch passage. This is a future lead, not a detour required for the rescue.
- Rival crews value access more than a pile of dead Ravens. A temporary agreement is possible without permanent friendship.

## Fail-Forward Notes

Before a risky roll, agree on the concrete danger. Use relevant existing Dragonbane skills and rules; this guide introduces no new automatic injuries or conditions.

- **Failed navigation:** Reach the right junction by a worse approach, losing time or exposing your route.
- **Failed lifting or shoring:** The opening is made, but a support shifts; stop and brace it or proceed with a clearly stated collapse risk.
- **Failed negotiation:** The crew names an unacceptable price or alerts others. Access remains possible through another approach.
- **Failed medical aid:** The Raven still needs carrying and cannot contribute to the extraction; apply ordinary injury consequences where appropriate.
- **Failed stealth:** Rivals learn someone is moving through their territory; pursuit, a demand or a confrontation becomes possible, not inevitable combat.

Do not charge time, equipment loss and injury for the same failure by default. Choose the consequence established beforehand.

## If Nobody Intervenes

The messenger recruits labourers, while Thabor attempts the higher service opening. The chamber floods, heavy gear and bodies are lost, and rivals may become the rescuers. Decide the survivors' eventual fate from the established dangers and any off-screen resolution method you normally use—not an assumption that refusal must kill Thabor.

The Ravens lose influence, the district suffers disruption and whoever provides aid gains a claim on the survivors. These developments can return later without punishing the players for pursuing another urgent thread.

## Outcomes and Follow-Up

- **Successful extraction:** The survivors owe the actual rescuers; ask what, if anything, the party wants in return.
- **Official operation:** [[Reindell Hathor]] gains a report, the district is cordoned and access may be regulated.
- **Rival assistance:** A temporary truce or debt complicates the Ravens' ambitions below the city.
- **New discovery:** The exposed old passage becomes a separate expedition once the rescue is over.

End on what the party changed: people saved, relationships altered and a newly accessible piece of the Underlanes. No predetermined death, betrayal or mandatory monster encounter is needed.
