#### The Stone Prow
- [[Altes Lesym]] explains his variant of Power Source. He knows the method but cannot supply the power himself, as most of it resides within the book.
- [[Ariel Enel]] supplies and stabilises the power, drawing the overlapping realities into a boundary that can be sealed. The ritual must take place at the highest point, nearest the tear.
- [[Clawbert Danfangle]] channels the ritual, then speaks **Seal** to close the prepared boundary. The others defend them and maintain the ritual's anchors.
- Together they can close this tear, but cannot heal the wider Veil. The Reverie may still bleed through afterwards.

**Ariel's warning:**
> "We must draw enough power to give the breach an edge. Until he closes it, everything beyond will see us."

Give the party time to choose positions, prepare defences and establish a way to anchor Ariel. Explain the progress and instability tracks before the ritual begins. Protecting Ariel does not require forgiving her.

#### The Ritual 

##### Running the Waves

- During waves 1 and 2, Clawbert may spend his **action once per round** to make a **CON roll** to channel the ritual.
- **Success:** mark 1 progress. Each wave requires **3 successes**, not necessarily consecutive.
- **Failure:** mark 1 instability. Existing progress is not lost.
- At 3 progress, advance the ritual and introduce the next wave. Reset progress, but **do not reset instability**.
- Defeating every enemy is not required to advance. Surviving enemies remain; defeating them early buys breathing room but does not complete the ritual.
- These intermediate rolls represent channelling, not repeated castings of Seal. Charge the Word's normal WP cost only for the final Seal.
- Other characters can defend the ritualists, maintain anchors, rescue Ariel or create a **boon on Clawbert's next attempt** through an appropriate action and check. Explain the likely benefit before they commit.

##### Wave 1 — Establish the Ritual

**Progress: 3/3** 2/3

The power becomes a beacon. Lesser spirits converge on the Court while Ariel holds the connection steady.

- **Objective:** protect the ritualists while Clawbert establishes the working.
- **Visible progress:** scattered magical light gathers into a defined seam; the storm begins turning towards it.
- At 3 successes, the ritual takes hold and the realms begin to overlap.

##### Wave 2 — Draw the Tear Together

**Progress: 3/3 1/3**

The Court distorts as the Reverie presses through. Memories, spirits and altered terrain complicate the defence; include a problem that cannot simply be killed.

- **Objective:** maintain the anchors while Clawbert draws the tear into a sealable boundary.
- **Visible progress:** the breach narrows, but the concentrated power attracts something greater.
- At 3 successes, the boundary is ready. The Hunger Demon forces its way through before Clawbert can finish.

##### Wave 3 — The Hunger / Arcane Horror Attack 3/3 0/3

The final creature is a Hunger Demon, once an Animist spirit of Purpose. Foreshadow its former nature through fragments of duty, direction or an impossible task it still attempts to complete.

- **Objective:** create an opening for Clawbert to speak the final Seal. There is no third three-success track.
- The Horror threatens the ritual itself. The party may fight it back, distract it or attempt to invoke its lost Purpose.
- Make the required opening clear in the fiction: the creature must be driven away from the seam or prevented from disrupting the final Word.
- Clawbert may help create that opening rather than spending every round rolling.

##### Instability — Ariel and the Veil

**Encounter-wide instability: [ ] [ ] [ ]**

1. **First failure — The Veil takes hold.** Ariel's shadow stretches into the breach and something catches it. She cannot leave the ritual's centre. Clearly warn the party that further instability could pull her through.
2. **Second failure — Ariel begins slipping through.** She is caught between realities. Give the party an opportunity to anchor or rescue her before another ritual attempt. Pulling her free destabilises the working and gives the enemies an opening, but does not erase earned progress.
3. **Third failure — Ariel is pulled beyond.** Unless the party has successfully secured her, she is dragged into the Reverie. If this happens before the final Seal, her connection continues to sustain the prepared boundary from the other side; the party can still finish, but doing so will strand her there.

**Rescue must matter:** successful anchoring changes the third-failure outcome; it is not merely a delay before an inevitable disappearance. Before the next attempt, explain whether the anchor will hold and what is now at risk. Further failures threaten those anchors or expose defenders rather than automatically overriding a successful rescue.

Let Ariel speak and potentially urge them to finish, but leave the decision to the players. Her disappearance is not confirmed death.

#### Sealing the Veil

Once the party creates an opening, Clawbert spends his action to speak **Seal**, making the Word's normal **CON roll** and paying **1 WP**.

- **Success:** the prepared boundary seals under his control.
- **Failure:** the Word still manifests, following the Scelus's principle that failure means loss of control rather than no effect. Mark instability and apply one of the Word's normal failure consequences. Resolve Ariel's fate using the anchors and rescue actions already established; do not negate them with an unavoidable sacrifice.
- This is the climax, not another three-success phase. The tear closes; the roll determines control and its cost.

If Ariel is beyond the Veil when it closes, show her alive on the other side for one heartbeat before the seam disappears. She is lost in the Reverie, opening a possible rescue thread.

End with unmistakable relief: the storm eases, the spirits recede and the Court becomes solid again. Leave one unsettling trace of the Reverie.

**The catastrophe has been stopped. This tear is sealed, but the wider Veil remains wounded.**
